# Reading pedagogy evidence review

Date: 2026-10-03. Checks the citations in `GDD.md` and adds the Finnish and Norwegian evidence base. Population: Norwegian bokmål children aged 4-7 using an app at home.

## Verdict

**Qualified support.** The core of the design is well supported by meta-analyses, Finnish practice and Norwegian guidance: systematic letter-sound teaching, letter sounds instead of letter names, continuant-first connected blending, and letters introduced early. Four supporting claims are weaker than the first draft implied:

- **Tracing.** In James & Engelhardt 2012, the reading circuit of 5-year-olds was recruited only after free handwriting, not after typing or tracing.
- **Solo reading apps.** The GraphoGame meta-analysis (McTigue et al. 2020, 19 studies) found no overall effect on word reading (g = -0.02), and g = 0.48 when an adult was closely involved.
- **Adaptivity.** In a Norwegian group-randomised trial, an adaptive and a non-adaptive version of the computer component worked equally well (Solheim et al. 2018).
- **Praise type.** Mueller & Dweck 1998 failed a close replication (Li & Bates 2019, n = 624).

The verdict would become well supported with a preregistered randomised trial of a home-use bokmål app for ages 4-7 that shows reading gains without adult involvement. No such trial was found.

Each source is marked VERIFIED (citation confirmed online on 2026-10-03; "[metadata-only]" means the content could not be read), or UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Finnish evidence

- **How Finland teaches first reading.** Synthetic phonics: children learn the letter-sound connections and assemble sounds into words. Syllables are a stage in decoding because Finnish words are long; phonological awareness moves from syllables to phonemes. About one third of children decode at school entry at age 7 (Lyytinen, Richardson & Aro 2019, Cambridge University Press chapter, doi:10.1017/9781108553377.006).
- **The curriculum leaves the method to teachers.** Grades 1-2 aim to "learn to read and practice the necessary concepts in sound, letter, syllable, word". One year of pre-primary education at age 6 is compulsory (Leino & Sirén, PIRLS 2021 Encyclopedia: Finland). The EDUFI pre-primary curriculum itself was not read.
- **Hyphenated syllables are contested.** In an eye-tracking study of grade 1-2 readers, hyphenation gave no benefit for new words (Häikiö & Luotojärvi 2021, *Scientific Studies of Reading*, doi:10.1080/10888438.2021.1874384).
- **The Jyväskylä dyslexia study** followed 108 children with family risk of dyslexia and 92 controls from birth. Letter knowledge, phonological skills and rapid naming at ages 2.5-3.5 predicted later reading (Lohvansuu et al. 2021, *Brain Sciences* 11(4):427).
- **GraphoGame, the Finnish letter-sound game:**
  - Saine et al. 2011, *Child Development*: a positive developer-led trial, 25 children per group, inside a remedial package.
  - McTigue, Solheim, Zimmer & Uppstad 2020, *Reading Research Quarterly* 55(1):45-73, doi:10.1002/rrq.256: g = -0.02 overall on word reading, g = 0.48 with high adult interaction (abstract read 2026-10-03).
  - EEF/NFER England trial, 398 pupils: no evidence of an effect.
  - Lassault et al. 2022, *Scientific Studies of Reading* 26(6):449-468, France, N = 921: small effects, 0.18-0.27.
  - A Norwegian version was made with Lesesenteret. No Norwegian efficacy trial was found.
- **Limits of the Finnish model.** Finland scored 490 in PISA 2022 reading, 30 points below 2018. Seymour, Aro & Erskine 2003 (*British Journal of Psychology* 94(2):143-174) places Finnish alone as the simplest syllable structure with the shallowest spelling. Norwegian has complex syllables (clusters, closed syllables, sk/skj/kj, ng), so Finnish pacing will not transfer fully (my inference from that classification).

## 3. Norwegian evidence and teacher acceptance

- **Udir curriculum (Norwegian subject).** Aims after grade 2 include "leke med rim og rytme og lytte ut språklyder og stavelser i ord", "trekke bokstavlyder sammen til ord under lesing og skriving" and "skrive tekster for hånd og med tastatur". The retrieved PDF is version NOR01-06, marked expired; NOR01-07 was not read.
- **Lesesenteret, Språkløyper "Når elever strever med å lese ord".**
  - Start with two-letter words, then CVC words (sol, mus, lur), with continuants first (m, s), then stops.
  - "Bruk bokstavenes lyd og ikke bokstavnavnet."
  - Oral-only blending is "lite hensiktsmessig"; tie blending to written letters.
  - Syllables come later, as a strategy for long words.
  - Speed-based word apps "bør brukes med omhu, og kun i korte sekvenser"; accuracy before speed.
- **On Track (Lesesenteret).** A group-randomised trial in 13 schools: 744 children screened, 140 at risk, effect sizes 0.57-0.75 on reading and spelling. The intervention was teacher-led, with a computer letter-sound part, and the adaptive and non-adaptive versions did not differ (Solheim et al. 2018, *Learning and Instruction*, doi:10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.05.004, abstract read 2026-10-03). A 42-school replication succeeded with smaller effects (Solheim et al. 2024, *Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness*, doi:10.1080/19345747.2024.2333734).
- **Lervåg et al. 2018**, *Child Development*, doi:10.1111/cdev.12861: listening comprehension plus decoding explained 96% of the variance in early reading comprehension.
- **Acceptance risk.** The mandate for the government's reading commission (Lesekommisjonen) ties reading policy to "mindre skjerm, mer lek". Teachers will accept a screen app only if sessions are short and play-based and point children to reading offline.

## 4. Design changes adopted from this review

| Design decision | Evidence | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed systematic letter-sound order | Ehri et al. 2001 (meta-analysis); Udir; Lyytinen 2019 | Keep |
| Letters from the second episode; short listening world | Ehri et al. 2001 RRQ; Språkløyper | Keep; shorten the listening world |
| Letter sounds, not letter names | Språkløyper | Keep |
| Connected blending, continuants first | Gonzalez-Frey & Ehri 2021 (n = 38); Språkløyper | Keep; evidence is one small trial |
| Tracing each letter | James & Engelhardt 2012; Longcamp 2005 | **Changed:** Pip models the letter, then the child writes it from memory; tracing is only the last hint. Parents get a paper handwriting prompt |
| Adaptive engine (BKT) | Corbett & Anderson 1995; Solheim 2018 | Keep for pacing; claim no learning gain from adaptivity |
| Solo app | McTigue 2020; EEF; Lassault 2022 | **Changed:** adult co-play is part of the design (daily read-to-a-grown-up step, parent script); expected effects are small |
| Syllable clapping | Udir; Lyytinen 2019 | Keep; add syllable chunking for two-syllable words (le-se); never hyphenate shown words by default (Häikiö 2021) |
| Clusters and multi-letter graphemes | Seymour 2003; Språkløyper | **Added:** explicit islands for sk/skj/kj, ng and the two o sounds |
| No stars, coins, loot | Lepper 1973; Deci 1999 | Keep |
| Informational praise | Deci 1999 | Keep; cite Deci, not Dweck |
| Montessori framing | Randolph 2023 (g = 0.24); Lillard 2006 | Materials are design inspiration, not evidence |
| Movement activities | Mavilidi 2015 (vocabulary) | Keep for engagement; claim no letter-sound learning |
| No speed timers | Språkløyper | **Added** as a rule |
| Session length | Lesekommisjonen mandate | **Added:** session cap and offline follow-ups |

**Kahoot! Learn to Read by Poio.** A Norwegian app (founded 2012, bought by Kahoot in 2019), with Norwegian bokmål among its languages. A troll stole the storybook of the Readlings, letter creatures; the child unlocks letters, builds words and the words fill the storybook, which the child then reads to family. It uses coins, collectable cards and a subscription, and is marketed as needing no parental support. No independent study of its effect was found (search 2026-10-03). - **Borrowed:** the child's own words build the final storybook; letters as creatures with their own voice; freeing captured sounds as the story premise; no negative feedback; a demo on first use of every activity.
- **Avoided:** coins and decoration shops, the "learns alone" pitch, cages and a dark tone, subscriptions and in-app purchases, countdowns, points for speed and leaderboards.

## 7. Capital or small letters first (added 2026-10-03)

No randomised trial tests which case to teach first. The evidence is correlational plus one small experiment:

- Children know capitals earlier (Worden & Boettcher 1990, n = 188, doi:10.1080/10862969009547711, abstract only).
- A child is more than 16 times as likely to know a small letter if they know its capital (Turnbull, Bowles, Skibbe, Justice & Wiggins 2010, n = 461, doi:10.1044/1092-4388(2010/09-0093), abstract read 2026-10-03). Letters that look alike in both cases (o/O, s/S) are easier than a/A or g/G.
- Teaching capitals first did not speed up learning the small letters (Park & Piasta 2026, n = 36, *Annals of Dyslexia*, doi:10.1007/s11881-025-00360-z, abstract read 2026-10-03).
- Small letters b/d/p/q are mirror images of other letters and need their own practice (Fischer & Luxembourger 2021, doi:10.3389/fcomm.2021.719652).
- Practice: Norwegian schools teach "store og små bokstaver parallelt" (Holen skole, Bergen); Finland's education agency OPH says learn both at the same time; one Finnish primer starts with capitals. Udir LK20 does not say. Montessori sandpaper letters are lowercase (practitioner source only).

**Decision:** every letter is introduced as a pair (capital and small letter together, "Store S og lille s"). All reading, writing and word tasks use small letters, because running bokmål text is almost all lowercase. Capitals are used for real purposes: the child's name, names, and the start of a sentence. b/d/p/q get separate discrimination practice and are introduced far apart.
